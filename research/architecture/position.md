# Position: where the review ends and the work begins

What the field has shown, what these searches did not find, and what this repository asks next.

**Shape:** [position](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/master/shapes/position.md)

## Known

- Protection principles older than the agents they now govern name what a gate does: mediate every access, grant least privilege, fail closed (Saltzer 1975; Abbott 1976).
- Model-side priority can be trained, and harness-side context assembly can undo it (Wallace 2024; He 2026).
- Guardrails over agent actions are measured, and their error rates move with the surface form of the action (Xiang 2024; Demirkiran 2026).
- A propose-and-verify loop with sound critics carries a guarantee that follows the critics (Kambhampati 2024; Gundawar 2024).
- Repository tests judge real issues, and cascades with per-step routing cut cost at near-kept quality (Jimenez 2024; Chen 2023; Paul 2026).

## Not found in these searches

- F2, arxiv, guardrails for LLM agents: no survey of controlled agent architectures as a whole.
- F10 and F11 failed, so Semantic Scholar and OpenAIRE, two engines of the plan, were never searched.
- No measurement of a prose rule against an enforced monitor, and none of isolated worktrees for coding agents.

## What this repository asks

- Does the gate change what jobs pass, or only what they cost, on the same ledger?
- How often does the cheapest allowed rung pass per class, and what does the second attempt add?
- Can a learned router beat the class table at the same share cap, on cost per passed job?

## What the shapes must decide

- Whether a private object may anchor a review whose every other source is public.
- Whether the grey scores need a second rater, and whether workshop preprints may hold records.
